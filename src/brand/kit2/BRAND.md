SheFreelances AI teaches women how to start a freelance marketing business: learn one sellable skill, package it as an offer, and land the first clients. The brand should feel like a smart friend who has already done it: warm, plain-spoken and confident, with the polish of a good magazine. It is the starting step. Freelancer to CEO (F2CEO) is the next step once she has clients.

**This is Brand 2.0**, set by the October 2026 landing page refresh: midnight sections with real photos of Suzy, a five-color gradient used as a few bright accents, pill buttons and motion that rewards scrolling. It replaces the flat, no-gradient rules of 1.0 for everything public (site, funnels, ads, slides). The logged-in app still follows its own rules in `app-ui.md` until it gets its own refresh.

## Who we're talking to

Women 25 to 44 who are early-stage freelancers or want to be. She might be planning to leave a corporate job, building side income around a full-time job, or working from home while raising kids. She is capable and busy. She is skeptical of "make money online" promises and has probably been burned by one. She wants a clear path, a skill she can sell, and proof that people like her are doing it.

## The look in one line

Midnight and paper, a little gradient, and Suzy. Dark `night` sections alternate with soft-white `surface` sections, the gradient shows up in a few named places, and real photos of Suzy at Google, with her family and working from anywhere carry the trust.

## Content fundamentals

- **Voice:** warm, direct, practical. Write like a mentor who respects her time: short sentences, concrete next steps, no fluff.
- **Person:** speak to "you". Use "we" for the program and community. Use "I" in founder-voice content (the teacher section, the story timeline).
- **Casing:** sentence case for every headline, button and nav item. Kickers (eyebrows) are the only uppercase text. The handwritten note is lowercase.
- **Headlines in the step and story sections have no trailing period** ("Learn a skill", "Raising 3 kids").
- **No em dashes** in brand copy. Use a period, a comma or a colon.
- **No "it's not X, it's Y" lines**, and no "not just X, but Y". Say the positive thing directly.
- **Plain language first.** Define every marketing term the first time it appears: "CPM (what you pay for 1,000 ad views)".
- **Results claims carry the disclaimer.** Suzy's own numbers ("generated over $1B for brands", "we just crossed $2M!") are hers to state. Never promise the reader income, and keep the footer line: "Individual results vary. No specific income or client-acquisition outcomes are guaranteed."
- **Say:** first client, your offer, a skill you can sell, side income, work from anywhere, step by step, you're not behind.
- **Don't say:** boss babe, girlboss, hustle, passive income, six figures fast, quit your job tomorrow, unlock, supercharge, game-changer, "AI does it for you". AI is a tool she learns to use.

## Name and wordmark

- Write the name exactly **SheFreelances AI**: one word "SheFreelances", a space, then capital "AI". Never "She Freelances", "Shefreelances" or "SFAI" in public copy.
- On night, the wordmark is "She" in `lavender`, "Freelances" in `cream`, both Gloock, and "AI" in a small `cream` tag with navy text. On paper use the Logos files (`brand-text` She, `ink` Freelances, `butter` AI tag).
- The Sf monogram goes anywhere small: profile pictures, favicon, app icon. Never stretch, recolor, outline or add effects to a logo file.

## Color

Two grounds, one gradient, one pink.

- **Grounds.** Public pages alternate `night` (hero, teacher, toolkit, How it works, final call to action, footer) and `surface` (Everything you get, the course, Never freelanced, FAQ). Never put two paper sections apart with a single dark one in a way that leaves a dark sliver; give each section real height. Panels on night use `night-raised`; white cards on paper use `panel` with `lift-paper` and a visible `line` border so they never blend in.
- **Text.** `cream` on night, `ink` on paper. Secondary text is `on-night-muted` on night and `ink-muted` on paper. Emphasized words inside a headline are `lavender` on night (for example "leave Google", "it works").
- **The spectrum** (`spectrum-coral` → `spectrum-pink` → `spectrum-violet` → `spectrum-periwinkle`, with `spectrum-sky` only at the far end of long lines) only ever appears as the gradient. Use `grad-spectrum` in these places and nowhere else:
  - two or three words of a headline: the hero's "with AI", the course's "...and land clients fast.", the final "freelance business?", the price "Free"
  - thin lines: the How it works light, the story timeline, the hero video border
  - rings: lit step bubbles, the open FAQ card, the play button, the "course is free" box (`grad-spectrum-diagonal`)
  - soft blurred glows (`grad-glow`) behind the hero video, the portrait and the final photo
- **Never** fill a section, a card or a button with the bright gradient, and never use it on body text. If a section already has a gradient headline, its other accents go quiet.
- **Hot pink** is the one solo accent. The Meet your teacher button uses `pink-cta` (#e0428a, a touch deeper than the gradient's pink so its white label reads). Eyebrow dashes and the pull-quote bar use `spectrum-pink`.
- **The dark gradient** (`grad-dark`, `deep-indigo` → `deep-plum` → `deep-berry`) is the quiet accent on paper: check circles in the course, the Get paid chip, the block behind the FAQ photo. `grad-dark-button` is for a button on paper that should feel special without going pink.
- **Lavender and butter** stay from 1.0: `lavender` for the wordmark "She" and emphasized words on night, `butter` for the AI tag. `success` and `danger` are states only, always with a word.

## Typography

- **Display face:** Gloock (Google Fonts), regular weight only, never bold. `hero` (92px, tight -0.03em) once per page, `section-title` (62px) for section heads, `step-title` (27px) for steps and toolkit cards, and `display` / `h1` / `h2` as before.
- **Text face:** the device's system font. Body 17 to 19px on marketing pages at 1.55 line height. `story-caption` (15px semibold) for timeline chapter titles and captions: sans, never Gloock.
- **Kickers:** `kicker`, uppercase, 0.14em tracking, with a 24px dash before it in `lavender-deep` (or `spectrum-pink` in pink-accent sections).
- **The handwritten note:** Caveat 600 (`note`), lowercase, a few words on a cream paper tag taped to a photo ("my first day!"). One per page.
- Two-line headlines break where the meaning breaks ("Learn the freelancing skills that clients pay for" / "...and land clients fast.").

## Shape

- **Buttons are pills** (`radius-pill`), 56 to 62px tall on marketing pages, label plus a → arrow that nudges on hover. Light pill (`cream`, navy text) on night; navy pill on paper; hot-pink pill once in the teacher section; `grad-dark-button` once in the course section.
- Large panels use `radius-panel` (30px), FAQ and step cards `radius-card` (20px), timeline photos `radius-photo` (5px).
- Corners on photos stay crisp. The FAQ photo is square-cut with a `grad-dark` block offset 22px behind it, never rounded, never faded at the edges.

## Iconography

- **Gradient icons** (Gradient icons group): 24px grid, 1.7 stroke, each drawn in its own slice of the spectrum, always on a `navy` tile (56px, 16px corners). Used for the seven things she gets and the three "Maybe..." lines. Inline the SVG so the gradient renders.
- **Line icons** (Icons group) stay for UI chrome in the app: `currentColor`, 1.75 stroke.
- No emoji, no Lucide, no sparkle icons next to "AI".

## Imagery

Real photos of Suzy carry the brand. No stock photos, no illustrations of people, no mockups of fake dashboards.

- **Hero:** the video, in a frame with a gradient border, rising from tilted to flat as you scroll.
- **Teacher:** one large portrait (`radius-panel`) with the two proof badges ("Ex-Google Marketing exec", "$1B+ generated for global brands"), then the story timeline: Ex-Google Marketing Executive (Google sign with the "my first day!" note, the Google stage), Raising 3 kids (couple, family on the beach), Own a 7 fig business and work from anywhere (Alaska, Vegas, the ski slope). Chapters stagger above and below a gradient line; photos are 4:5, cropped from the top so no head is cut.
- **Background photo:** the speaking photo sits behind How it works at about a third strength, faded to night on the left and edges, moving slower than the page.
- **FAQ:** one photo, square corners, `grad-dark` block offset behind it.
- **Final call to action:** one straight (never tilted) photo of Suzy working on her laptop, with a soft `grad-glow` behind it.
- Product screenshots appear as the toolkit mockup and the wireframe tool cards, never as fake dashboards.

## Motion

Motion rewards scrolling and never blocks reading.

- **Video rise:** the hero video starts tilted back (about 34° on desktop, 20° on phones) and rises flat as the page scrolls; its glow strengthens as it lands.
- **The light-up line:** in How it works a white dot with a gradient trail runs along the line as you scroll, and each numbered bubble gets a gradient ring as the light reaches it.
- **Parallax:** the How it works background photo moves at about 12% of the scroll speed, clamped so it never slides past the top edge.
- **Buttons:** the arrow nudges 3px right on hover and the button lifts 2px. No other hover motion.
- **Pop-up form:** every "Get free access" button opens the sign-up form in a pop-up with a gradient top bar.
- **Reduced motion:** when the viewer has it on, the video sits flat, the steps show fully lit, the photo stays still and nothing loops.

It follows "Gallery by day, Midnight by night" in `app-ui.md` with styles in `app.css`: off-white page, white panels, midnight sidebar and midnight AI tool cards, square-ish corners, no gradients.
